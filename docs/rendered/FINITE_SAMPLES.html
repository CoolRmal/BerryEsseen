The finite-sample argument

The analytic reduction in this note and its application to all saved finite-sample numerical records have passed Lean. The formulas below identify exactly what those records prove.

Let X have mean zero, variance one, and third absolute moment β. Write f(u) = E exp(iuX). A parameter cell specifies

1≤N≤n≤H,1≤L≤β≤B,c>0,0<τ≤12.1\le N\le n\le H,\qquad 1\le L\le\beta\le B,\qquad c>0,\qquad 0<\tau\le\tfrac12.1≤N≤n≤H,1≤L≤β≤B,c>0,0<τ≤21​.

The aim is to bound the normalized CDF error for every law and every integer sample size in this cell. We choose the smoothing cutoff

Tn=cn.T_n=c\sqrt n.Tn​=cn​.

This choice makes the single-summand frequency T_n s / sqrt(n) = cs independent of n. Consequently, the same functions of u=cs can be used throughout the cell.

1. Bounds for one summand

Choose functions M(u) and E(u) with

∣f(u)∣≤M(u)≤1,∣f(u)−e−u2/2∣≤u3E(u),E(u)≥0.|f(u)|\le M(u)\le1,\qquad |f(u)-e^{-u^2/2}|\le u^3 E(u),\qquad E(u)\ge0.∣f(u)∣≤M(u)≤1,∣f(u)−e−u2/2∣≤u3E(u),E(u)≥0.

The modulus bound is needed on the whole smoothing interval. The error bound is needed only on 0<s<τ. All moment-dependent bounds are evaluated at the upper endpoint B; their monotonicity in the third moment justifies this substitution.

The two error coefficients used by the records are

Eclassical(u)=B6+u8,E_{\rm classical}(u)=\frac B6+\frac u8,Eclassical​(u)=6B​+8u​,

and, for 0≤u≤π,

Eanchor(u)=(B−16+uQ(u2))2+(B−1)(B+5/3)36.E_{\rm anchor}(u)= \sqrt{\left(\frac{B-1}{6}+uQ(u^2)\right)^2 +\frac{(B-1)(B+5/3)}{36}}.Eanchor​(u)=(6B−1​+uQ(u2))2+36(B−1)(B+5/3)​​.

Here Q is the explicit polynomial in GaussianPolynomial.lean, satisfying

0≤e−u2/2−cos⁡u≤u4Q(u2).0\le e^{-u^2/2}-\cos u\le u^4Q(u^2).0≤e−u2/2−cosu≤u4Q(u2).

For the anchored error, the real part is bounded by u³((B−1)/6+uQ(u²)), and the imaginary part by u³ sqrt((B−1)(B+5/3))/6. Squaring and adding proves the displayed bound. The nonnegative factor u³ can be taken outside the square root. These steps, including u=0, are proved in FiniteSampleBounds.lean.

The available smooth modulus branches are

Mline(u)=1−u2+(B+1)u3/5,(B+1)u≤9/2,M_{\rm line}(u)=\sqrt{1-u^2+(B+1)u^3/5}, \qquad (B+1)u\le9/2,Mline​(u)=1−u2+(B+1)u3/5​,(B+1)u≤9/2,
Mcos(u)=1−2a(1−cos⁡((B+1)u))(B+1)2,9/2≤(B+1)u≤2π,M_{\rm cos}(u)= \sqrt{1-\frac{2a(1-\cos((B+1)u))}{(B+1)^2}}, \qquad 9/2\le(B+1)u\le2\pi,Mcos​(u)=1−(B+1)22a(1−cos((B+1)u))​​,9/2≤(B+1)u≤2π,

where a = 81/(80(1−cos(9/2))), and the constant bound M=1. An anchored modulus branch is

Manchor(u)=(∣cos⁡u∣+(B−1)u3/6)2+I(u)2,M_{\rm anchor}(u)= \sqrt{\left(|\cos u|+(B-1)u^3/6\right)^2+I(u)^2},Manchor​(u)=(∣cosu∣+(B−1)u3/6)2+I(u)2​,

where I can be any of the three proved imaginary-part bounds. To evaluate this branch as a smooth function on a panel, its cosine sign is fixed and certified, and the expression under the square root is checked to be at most one. Square-root derivative evaluation also requires a strictly positive argument. FiniteModulus.lean proves the branch inequalities with these domain conditions explicit.

2. The geometric sum and the sample-size interval

Put

Gk(m,g)=∑j=0k−1mjgk−1−j.G_k(m,g)=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}m^j g^{k-1-j}.Gk​(m,g)=j=0∑k−1​mjgk−1−j.

For any positive integer k,

Gk(m,g)k=∫01((1−v)g+vm)k−1 dv.(A)\frac{G_k(m,g)}k =\int_0^1\big((1-v)g+vm\big)^{k-1}\,dv. \tag{A}kGk​(m,g)​=∫01​((1−v)g+vm)k−1dv.(A)

For m≠g, integrate the derivative of ((1−v)g+vm)^k and use (m−g)G_k(m,g)=m^k−g^k. For m=g, both sides equal m^(k−1). This proves (A) without excluding equal bases or zero bases.

If 0≤m,g≤1, the integrand decreases when k increases. Thus, for N≤n≤H,

Gn(m,g)≤nNGN(m,g)≤HNGN(m,g).(B)G_n(m,g)\le\frac nN G_N(m,g)\le\frac HN G_N(m,g). \tag{B}Gn​(m,g)≤Nn​GN​(m,g)≤NH​GN​(m,g).(B)

The factorization of a difference of powers now gives, with u=cs and g=e^(−u²/2),

∣f(u)n−gn∣≤HN u3E(u)GN(M(u),g).(C)\left|f(u)^n-g^n\right| \le \frac HN\,u^3E(u)G_N(M(u),g). \tag{C}∣f(u)n−gn∣≤NH​u3E(u)GN​(M(u),g).(C)

Also, |f(u)|^n≤M(u)^N. The Gaussian power g^n equals exp(−T_n²s²/2), exactly the comparison function needed in smoothing. FiniteGeometricAverage.lean proves (A) and (B); FiniteSampleBounds.lean applies (C) to the actual iid law.

3. Smooth kernel bounds

Use the project's doubled positive-frequency Prawitz kernel K. Define q(x)=1/x−cot(x). On [0,π/2], the rational functions

q−(x)=xA−(x)D+(x),q+(x)=xA+(x)D−(x)q_-(x)=\frac{xA_-(x)}{D_+(x)},\qquad q_+(x)=\frac{xA_+(x)}{D_-(x)}q−​(x)=D+​(x)xA−​(x)​,q+​(x)=D−​(x)xA+​(x)​

satisfy 0≤q_-≤q≤q_+. The four polynomials are defined in CotangentPolynomials.lean. Their inequalities follow from signed Taylor bounds for sine and from sin(x)−x cos(x)=∫₀ˣ t sin(t) dt. The two denominators and the lower numerator are strictly positive on this entire closed interval; this is proved in FiniteKernels.lean.

Set

K0(s)=[s(1−s)]2+[1/π−s(1−s)q−(πs)]2,K_0(s)=\sqrt{[s(1-s)]^2+ [1/\pi-s(1-s)q_-(\pi s)]^2},K0​(s)=[s(1−s)]2+[1/π−s(1−s)q−​(πs)]2​,
KR(s)=(1−s)1+q+(π(1−s))2,KC(s)=(1−s)1+q+(πs)2.K_R(s)=(1-s)\sqrt{1+q_+(\pi(1-s))^2},\qquad K_C(s)=(1-s)\sqrt{1+q_+(\pi s)^2}.KR​(s)=(1−s)1+q+​(π(1−s))2​,KC​(s)=(1−s)1+q+​(πs)2​.

Then s|K(s)|≤K₀(s) for 0<s≤1/2, |K(s)|≤K_R(s) for 1/2≤s<1, and the normal-correction kernel has modulus at most K_C(s) for 0<s≤1/2. Multiplication by s in K₀ removes the low-frequency singularity.

5. What a normalized Taylor panel proves

On a panel [m−h,m+h], let G(t)=F(m+ht). Store enclosures for G^(k)(t)/k!, k=0,…,4. Suppose u₀,u₂ bound the zeroth and second coefficients at t=0, and u₄ bounds the fourth coefficient throughout −1≤t≤1. Signed Taylor remainder and change of variables give

∫m−hm+hF(s) ds≤2h(u0+u23+u45).(E)\int_{m-h}^{m+h}F(s)\,ds \le2h\left(u_0+\frac{u_2}{3}+\frac{u_4}{5}\right). \tag{E}∫m−hm+h​F(s)ds≤2h(u0​+3u2​​+5u4​​).(E)

Odd terms integrate to zero. Coefficients may have either sign. In particular, the fourth coefficient must bound the whole panel, not only its midpoint.
